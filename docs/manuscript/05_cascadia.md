# The grove's Cascadia {#sec:cascadia}

The grove is invented; most of what lives in it is not. The field station and
the storybook take their weather, their animals and their unease from the
Pacific Northwest coast, and three pieces of the real region shape what the
games do.

Fog. Coast redwoods comb water out of the summer fog, and the drip that falls
beneath them is a measurable input of water to the forest and to the plants
that grow under it [@dawson1998]. Both games keep the fog: the field station
hides its distances in it, and the storybook lets drops fall through it.

Tremor. Beneath the Cascadia margin the subducting plate slips slowly, again
and again, in episodes that come with a faint seismic tremor
[@rogers2003]. In the field station the ground hums every so often: masts that
have been re-tensioned ring their chords, slack ones rattle, and the radio
reports in.

The great earthquake. In {{CASCADIA_QUAKE_DATE}} a great Cascadia earthquake
sent a tsunami across the Pacific to Japan, where its arrival was written down,
and those records date the earthquake and bound its size [@satake1996]. The
coast dropped, tidewater drowned the forests standing on it, and the dead
trunks of some still stand in the marshes [@atwater2005]. The field station's
ghost forest is one of these, with a tensegrity mast among its flooded roots.
