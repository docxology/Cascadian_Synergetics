# Introduction {#sec:introduction}

Synergetics measures space in tetrahedra. Fuller took the regular tetrahedron
as the unit of volume, the closest packing of equal spheres as the frame of
reference, and the vector equilibrium, a cuboctahedron, as the shape that
closest packing makes around one sphere with its {{IVM_MOVES}} neighbours
[@fuller1975]. The geometry is usually met on the page, in diagrams and in
tables of volumes. This project puts it in a place instead: a grove of coast
redwoods where an Office of Synergetics Services keeps space from growing
square, after the gallery of that name [@ossgallery].

The games keep one rule from the start: show, do not tell. There is no briefing
screen. The isotropic vector matrix appears around a ranger who stands still; a
brass counter clicks out the volume of a tetrahedron the player marks; a dial
at the Cathedral Ring follows the jitterbug as it breathes. Nobody explains any
of it. That rule brings a second one with it. If a game shows a whole-number
count or a volume without saying why, the count and the volume have to be
right, and the only way to know is to compute them with the code the game runs
and to test that code against an independent source.

The rest of the paper follows that pairing. The games are described in
[@sec:games]. The geometry they show, and the results it yields, are in
[@sec:geometry]. How the code is imported and held to its upstream sources is
in [@sec:imports]. The real places and events behind the grove are in
[@sec:cascadia], and how every claim here is tied to the code is in
[@sec:verification].
