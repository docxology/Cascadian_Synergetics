# The real Cascadia behind the grove

The grove is invented; most of what lives in it is not.

- **Coast redwoods** (*Sequoia sempervirens*) are the tallest trees alive. Their crowns comb water out of the summer fog, which drips to the ground below; the field station's mist and the storybook's falling drops come from that.
- **Family circles.** Redwoods sprout from the root crown of a fallen or cut parent, so rings of trunks stand around an empty centre. The Cathedral Ring is one.
- **Fire hollows.** Old redwoods survive fires that burn out their bases, leaving rooms inside the living tree (locally called goosepens). One holds a ranger's bedroll.
- **Albino redwoods.** A few hundred redwood shoots are known that lack chlorophyll and live off their parent tree, ghost-white among the green. One stands at the far edge of the field station.
- **Sword fern and redwood sorrel** carpet the floor. Sorrel's three-part leaves are the grove's commonest triangle.
- **Banana slugs** (*Ariolimax columbianus*) are bright yellow and grow to about 25 cm.
- **Chinook salmon** run upstream through coastal creeks.
- **The varied thrush** sings long, single, slightly buzzing whistles, each at a different pitch. It is the bird you hear most.
- **Marbled murrelets** are seabirds that nest high on the branches of old-growth conifers and fly inland at dawn (storybook).
- **Roosevelt elk** graze the coastal prairies and redwood edges (storybook).
- **Pacific rhododendron** flowers pink in the redwood understory (storybook).
- **Ghost forests.** On 26 January 1700 a great Cascadia Subduction Zone earthquake dropped stretches of the coast, and tidewater drowned the forests standing there. Silver trunks of western red cedar still stand in some of those marshes. The field station's ghost forest is one.
- **Fire lookouts** stand on Cascadian summits and ridges, their lamps the last lights in the fog.
- **Foghorns** sound from the coast, low and two-toned.
- **The visitor** at the fog's edge belongs to the region's Sasquatch lore, where two knocks on wood are a familiar sign.
